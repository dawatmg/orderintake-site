How it works

Three steps from your inbox to your ERP.

No integration to build, no software to install, no change to the ERP you already run. You point us at order documents; we hand back orders your system can import.

STEP 01

Point us at your order inbox

Forward a shared mailbox or drop a folder of the last 500 order documents — email bodies, PDFs, spreadsheets, scanned faxes, even voicemail recordings. Nothing is installed and no one logs into your ERP.

  • One shared mailbox or a zipped folder is enough to start
  • We hash the inbox before touching it, so nothing is lost or altered
  • Unsupported files are listed for you, never silently dropped
STEP 02

We parse, map and validate

Our engine converts each document into a canonical order, matches every line to your catalogue, customer and pricing, and scores its own confidence. Anything below your threshold is held back and reviewed by a human before it reaches you.

  • Confidence threshold is yours — 0.85 by default
  • Ambiguous lines are flagged for clarification, never guessed
  • We measure our own accuracy on a gold set and show you the run
STEP 03

You get ERP-ready orders & a daily reconciliation

Structured order files that import straight into your ERP using its own import template, plus a daily report reconciling what came in, what we returned, and what we were unsure about.

  • CSV/Excel columns matched to your ERP import format
  • Counts reconciled: documents in = orders out, no unexplained gap
  • Low-confidence queue and corrections are visible to you, not hidden

Questions

The things an operations manager asks first.

What exactly counts as an "order document"?

Anything a buyer sends you that contains an order: an email body typed freehand, a PDF purchase order, an Excel or CSV attachment, a scanned fax or photo of a handwritten PO, and voicemail recordings or transcripts. If your team currently reads it and types it, it is in scope. Some formats we cannot handle are listed back to you rather than dropped.

Which ERPs do you support?

The output is a structured file — canonical order JSON plus a CSV/Excel file whose columns are matched to your ERP's import template. In practice that covers mid-market systems such as QuickBooks, Sage 100/300, Epicor, Odoo, Acumatica, NetSuite, SAP Business One and Infor. Tell us your ERP in the pilot request and we map to its import format before we deliver. Hypothesis

Do you integrate directly with my ERP?

Not during the pilot — the pilot produces files, which keeps it zero-risk and reversible. Production starts with your ERP's own import format. Deeper API-level integration is the later embedded tier, and we only build it once the mapping library is proven on real orders.

Where does my data go, and is it safe?

Your documents stay on one encrypted machine and are not uploaded to any third-party AI, OCR, cloud drive or email service — the engine runs locally. Raw documents are deleted within 30 days of the pilot report being accepted; parsed orders and reports are kept 12 months for your dispute window. We hash your inbox before touching it so nothing is lost or altered. The full policy is written down in ops/DATA-HANDLING.md. ops/DATA-HANDLING.md

What happens when the engine is unsure?

Every order carries a confidence score. Lines below your threshold (0.85 by default) are held and reviewed by a person against the source document before they are released. Lines that genuinely cannot be resolved are marked for your clarification — we flag them, we never guess. The low-confidence queue is part of what you receive, not hidden from you.

How long does the pilot take?

Once the 500 documents are in our hands, we aim to return the accuracy report and the structured orders within a small number of business days. We confirm the exact turnaround when we agree the scope, and we tell you if a format in your set needs a closer look. Hypothesis

Do I have to change my ERP, or lose staff?

Neither. Your ERP stays exactly as it is — we produce the files it already imports. Your order-entry team stops typing and starts handling exceptions, which is the part that actually needs a person. The goal is fewer people on data entry, not fewer people.

What does it cost after the pilot?

Nothing until you choose to go to production. Production is $1,500–$4,000 per month covering up to 2,000 orders, then $0.35–$0.75 per order beyond that. You only move to it if the pilot proved the output posts into your ERP correctly.
